\title{Solver Sensitivity in Newton--Raphson Label Generation\\
\large Why the custom NR solver fails, and where it fails ---\\
evidence from 28 PPC benchmark grids}

\author{Changhun Kim}
\date{July 28, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Phase~0 backbone data generation for the PIGNN-Attn-LS Newton--Raphson (NR)
surrogate initially produced usable labels on only 12 of 28 benchmark grids.
The working hypothesis was that the $\pm40\%$ global load-scaling range of the
\texttt{backbone} preset was too aggressive.  A controlled sweep disproves
this: narrowing the range to $\pm20\%$ and $\pm10\%$ changed convergence by
less than $6\%$ on the affected grids, while holding the range fixed at
$\pm40\%$ and substituting the pandapower NR implementation for the in-house
solver moved the same grids from $0.3\%$ to $100\%$ convergence.  The
controlling variable is the solver, not the perturbation magnitude.  We
characterise two distinct failure regimes of the custom solver---an
ill-conditioning regime affecting distribution-type and tightly-constrained
grids at any size, and an absolute size wall above ${\sim}1{,}300$ buses where
convergence is identically zero---and report the full 28-grid regeneration,
which yields $909{,}216$ labelled samples with no job failures.
\end{abstract}


% ===================================================================
\section{Motivation and Hypothesis Under Test}
% ===================================================================

The \texttt{backbone} preset (Phase~0) applies a fixed Y-bus perturbation
family: global load scaling on $[0.60, 1.40]$, per-bus load jitter
($\sigma_P{=}0.10$, $\sigma_Q{=}0.15$), generator jitter ($\sigma_G{=}0.05$),
PV setpoint variation on $[0.95, 1.05]$\,pu, and randomised initial voltages.
Topology and admittance perturbation are disabled.

The initial campaign produced full $36{,}000$-row datasets on 12 grids and
little or nothing on the remaining 16.  Two explanations are consistent with
that observation:

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item[H1 (perturbation).] The $\pm40\%$ load range pushes samples outside the
  feasible operating region, so the labels legitimately do not exist.
\item[H2 (solver).] The samples are feasible, but the in-house NR
  implementation fails to converge to them.
\end{description}

These make opposite predictions.  Under H1, narrowing the load range must
raise the converged-sample count.  Under H2, narrowing the range changes
little, while changing the solver at fixed range changes a great deal.
Both experiments were run.

The failure is \emph{not} a simple monotone function of size.  Two separable
regimes appear.

\paragraph{Regime A --- ill-conditioning, size-independent.}
case33bw (33 buses) fails at $1.9\%$ while case118 (118 buses) succeeds
completely.  The grids in this regime are not the large ones; they are the
ones whose electrical characteristics stress a textbook NR formulation:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{case33bw} is a radial distribution feeder.  Distribution
    networks carry $R/X$ ratios an order of magnitude above transmission
    values, which is the classical failure mode for an unmodified NR
    Jacobian.
  \item \textbf{case89pegase}, \textbf{case145}, \textbf{iceland} and
    \textbf{case\_illinois200} are realistic or reduced-realistic models with
    tight reactive limits and narrow feasible regions, as opposed to the
    well-conditioned academic IEEE cases that surround them in the table.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Regime B --- absolute size wall above ${\sim}1{,}300$ buses.}
Every grid from case1354pegase upward returns \emph{exactly} zero converged
samples, without exception, across nine grids spanning $1{,}354$ to $9{,}241$
buses.  This is not partial degradation; the transition from case300
($42.3\%$) to case1354pegase ($0\%$) is a cliff.  The failure is also
expensive: divergent samples exhaust all $K{=}40$ iterations before being
discarded, so case6515rte consumed 17 array tasks $\times$ 24\,h and
GBnetwork 22\,h of wall time to produce nothing.  Logs show
\texttt{Jacobian singular, abort NR} and non-finite intermediates
(\texttt{invalid value encountered in exp}) in the update step.

\paragraph{Regime~B is entirely eliminated.}
All nine grids above $1{,}300$ buses that returned zero now return data, four
of them at or near $100\%$.  The size wall was an artefact of the solver.

\paragraph{Regime~A is almost entirely eliminated.}

exceeded a 24\,h wall clock completed in 30\,min--4\,h.

\section{The One Genuine Exception: case145}
% ===================================================================

case145 is the sole grid where H1 survives.  It improves only $2.5\times$
($3{,}202 \to 7{,}885$, $21.9\%$) and pandapower's own linear algebra reports
the failure explicitly:

\begin{center}
\texttt{newtonpf.py:512: MatrixRankWarning: Matrix is exactly singular}\\
\texttt{create\_jacobian.py:27: RuntimeWarning: invalid value encountered in divide}
\end{center}

Crucially, the run logged \emph{zero} \texttt{not-converged} events: the
solver is not failing to converge, it is encountering a structurally singular
Jacobian.  This is genuine numerical infeasibility of the perturbed operating
points rather than a solver deficiency, and it is the one case where
narrowing the load range is the correct remedy.  A $\pm20\%$ or $\pm10\%$
rerun of case145 alone is recommended.

The partial yields on the four largest grids ($50.8$--$71.0\%$) are of the
same character: at $6{,}000$--$9{,}241$ buses a $\pm40\%$ excursion places a
genuine fraction of samples outside the feasible set.  These datasets remain
usable at $18$k--$26$k rows; achieving a uniform $36$k would require
over-sampling (requesting ${\sim}60$k attempts to retain $36$k).

\section{Methodological Note: A Filename Collision}
\label{sec:collision}
% ===================================================================

The $\pm20\%$ entries missing from Experiment~1 were lost to a defect worth
recording, because it affects reproducibility of any future sweep.

The output path was constructed as
\[
\texttt{\{case\}\_\{mode\}\_\{scenario\_level\}\_\{start\_mode\}\_\{nr\_unit\}\_\{runs\}}
\]
which encodes \emph{neither} the load-scale range \emph{nor} the label
solver.  Consequently the $\pm20\%$, $\pm10\%$ and pandapower runs of a given
grid all resolved to the same path and were launched concurrently with
\texttt{--overwrite}.  Three jobs died with
\texttt{OSError: error closing file} from concurrent writes, and the
first-generation pandapower results were overwritten by later-finishing
$\pm10\%$ jobs; those $36{,}000$-row datasets survive only as log lines.

The filename now carries both fields, e.g.
\begin{center}
\small
\texttt{case118\_ppcY\_backbone\_dc\_compile\_ppNR\_ls0.60-1.40\_siNR\_36000\_...}
\end{center}
Verified: the four variants that previously collapsed onto one path now yield
four distinct names.  A companion defect in
\texttt{merge\_chunked\_parquets.py}---which matched chunk-directory prefixes
disjunctively and would therefore have blended stale zero-row custom-NR
chunks into a pandapower merge---was fixed to select exactly one chunk family
and log its choice.  All ten merges in the final campaign reported selecting
the \texttt{\_ppnr\_chunk\_} family, and chunk row-sums were verified equal to
merged row counts for all ten grids.

\section{Effect on Label Semantics}
% ===================================================================

Switching solvers does not alter the dataset schema or remove training
signal.  Both code paths write the same columns: \texttt{u\_start} (the
initial voltage guess, the model input) and \texttt{u\_newton\_si} /
\texttt{S\_newton\_si} (the converged solution, the label).  The dataset
stores the converged \emph{endpoint}, not the iteration trajectory, so the
learned line-search component---which lives in the model
(\texttt{GNSMsg\_armijo.py}), not in the data---is unaffected.  The only
quantity lost is the \texttt{nr\_iterations} diagnostic, which the pandapower
path reports as $0$.

Pandapower's solver is itself a Newton--Raphson method; it differs in
engineering robustness (PV/PQ limit switching, sparse LU factorisation,
better-conditioned Jacobian assembly), not in algorithm.  Using it as the
label oracle is therefore consistent with the premise of an NR surrogate, and
arguably preferable: it is the widely used reference implementation.

\section{Conclusions}
% ===================================================================

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{The perturbation design was never the problem.}  Reducing the
  load range four-fold changed convergence by ${<}6\%$ on affected grids.
  The $\pm40\%$ \texttt{backbone} range is retained unchanged.

\item \textbf{The custom NR solver has two failure regimes:} an
  ill-conditioning regime affecting radial/high-$R/X$ and tightly-constrained
  grids at any size, and an absolute wall above ${\sim}1{,}300$ buses where
  yield is identically zero across nine grids.

\item \textbf{Pandapower NR eliminates both regimes} at identical
  perturbation settings, converting 11 grids from zero or near-zero to
  usable, doubling the corpus to $909{,}216$ samples, covering all 28 grids,
  and running ${\sim}40\times$ faster.  The campaign completed 218 Slurm jobs
  with zero failures.

\item \textbf{One grid is genuinely infeasible at $\pm40\%$.}  case145
  exhibits a structurally singular Jacobian under both solvers and warrants a
  narrowed range; the four largest grids show partial infeasibility of the
  same kind.

\item \textbf{Recommendation.}  Adopt \texttt{pandapower\_nr} as the label
  oracle for all grids, retain $\pm40\%$ globally, rerun case145 at $\pm20\%$,
  and over-sample the four largest grids if uniform row counts are required.
  The custom-NR datasets are retained as the evidence base for this analysis
  and should be cited when justifying the solver choice in the methods
  section rather than presenting it as an arbitrary implementation detail.
\end{enumerate}
